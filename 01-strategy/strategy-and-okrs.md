# Product Strategy & OKRs: Fable Growth

> Module 1 · Write a Strategy That Can Survive a Test

## 1. Playing to Win cascade

| Question | Your choice |
|---|---|
| **Winning aspiration** | For existing Fable customers, success means they sustain their well being via the app by managing their emotional balance, improving sleep and continuing to reduce their stress levels, even when things are going well. |
| **Where to play** | Existing customers aged 25-45 who have already experienced Fable's benefits, but are starting to disengage because their original need has diminished.  I won't be focusing on newly acquired customers or those seeking specialised treatment or intervention. |
| **How to win** | Fable's potential competitive advantage is its ability to turn a trusted history of successful stress relief into personalised, preventative well being support.  Competitors could replicate existing features, but they cannot immediately replicate an established customer's history, trust and demonstrated experience of improvement with Fable. |
| **Capabilities required** | Match existing content and well being rituals to customers individual goals and preferences. |
| **Management systems** | We will  measure customer retention beyond 180 days, the levels of engagement of those customers continuing a well being journey & habit forming rituals across each quarter. |

## 2. Your one hard no

> **Hard no:** We will deliberately not invest in community-led engagement, despite its potential retention benefits. Instead, we will focus our limited resources on helping customers build sustainable, personalised well being habits.

## 3. OKR cascade

> **Objective:** Make Fable an everyday well being companion that customers continue to value beyond their initial period of stress.
>
> - **KR1:** Improve day-180 retention among post-acute users by 15% relative to baseline (Customer retention)
> - **KR2:** Increase day-180 premium subscription retention by 10% relative to baseline (Customer Value)
> - **KR3:** Increase Sustained Well being Value Rate by 15 percentage points versus control among post-acute users (Customer desirability)

## 4. AI pressure-test

**Most valid challenge:** The AI suggests that the biggest assumption is that post-acute users want ongoing well being support, and many users may associate Fable with that period and want to leave it behind. Our perceived advantage could be a reminder of something they’d rather forget.

**What I will change and what I will defend:** I'm going to defend the assumption that users won't or don't feel the need for ongoing well being support.  However I will change one of the key results relating to value rating as it appears ambiguous and could be gamified.

**Case Study:**
Fable · B2C
You're a Product Leader at Fable, a mental-wellness app for adults aged 25 to 45, built on guided meditations, sleep stories and breathing exercises. At its peak it had 4.2 million active users. Growth has now flatlined: people turn to Fable during a hard stretch, feel better within 90 to 120 days, and stop opening the app. The board has approved budget for one major initiative, and the clinical, growth and content teams each want a different bet.
The challenge: Fable's success is causing its churn. You define what Fable becomes for people after the acute phase, and how to keep high-value users engaged beyond their first 90 days.
Your goal: lead the Fable Growth initiative and build a board-ready case for where Fable should play, how it can win, and what it will take to get there.
